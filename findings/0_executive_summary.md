## Executive Summary

The ecommerce business experienced a significant slowdown after reaching peak monthly revenue in April 2026. Revenue declined sharply in May and remained weak through the first half of June, while average order value (AOV) also decreased. Customer concentration analysis indicates that power buyers generate the majority of reported revenue, highlighting both a valuable retention opportunity and a potential dependency risk. Together, these findings suggest that the business should prioritize restoring order volume, improving repeat purchases, and protecting high-value customer relationships.

Revenue deceleration: Monthly revenue fell from $6.73M in April to $3.80M in May, a 43.54% decline. June generated $1.02M over its first 16 days, equivalent to approximately $63.8K in daily revenue.
High customer concentration: 1,460 power buyers (14.60% of the stated 10,000-customer base) contributed $9.64M, or 63.45% of reported customer-segment revenue. Retaining these customers is critical to revenue stability.
Retention opportunity: 3,366 one-time buyers (33.66% of the customer base) contributed $2.15M, or 14.12% of reported revenue. Converting a portion of these customers into repeat buyers could increase customer lifetime value and reduce reliance on power buyers. Reported AOV also declined from $646.80 in April to $609.90 in June.

Recommendation: Prioritize recovery of completed-order volume while protecting AOV and high-value customer retention. For Q4, test a targeted reallocation of marketing spend from high-cost acquisition campaigns toward second-order email workflows, personalized cross-sell, and VIP loyalty incentives. Evaluate these initiatives using incremental repeat-purchase rate, revenue, customer retention, and contribution margin. Before finalizing Board-level conclusions, reconcile the 3,376 customers missing from the supplied segment breakdown and confirm that monthly and segment-level revenue figures use consistent definitions and reporting periods.
